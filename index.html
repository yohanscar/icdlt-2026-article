Verifiable Organizational Identity framework for Carbon Credit Registries Using ENS and DNSSEC

Wagner Yohan Scaramuzza Silva · Arlindo F. da Conceição

Federal University of São Paulo (UNIFESP)

2026

Abstract

This paper proposes a decentralized organizational identity framework for multi-chain carbon credit registries based on DNSSEC and the Ethereum Name Service (ENS). The framework establishes verifiable bindings between authoritative institutional domains and authorized blockchain wallets, providing an auditable identity layer for carbon-market participants operating across multiple blockchain networks. Two adoption models are investigated: a lightweight domain-wallet binding and a smart-contract-based organizational wallet registry supporting registration, revocation, and role-oriented wallet management. A proof-of-concept implementation was developed and evaluated through on-chain gas measurements. The results show deployment, registration, and revocation costs of 1.44M, 58K, and 67K gas, respectively. The evaluation demonstrates the technical feasibility of domain-backed organizational wallet attribution while identifying the assumptions and limitations associated with cross-chain state usage, governance, and asset-level integrity mechanisms beyond the scope of this work.

Introduction

The global effort to combat climate change has led to the development of various mechanisms, among which the carbon credit market stands out as a potentially powerful tool. However, this market faces significant challenges that threaten its efficacy and credibility \cite{iosco2023}.

Credible carbon markets face threats to their integrity, including double counting, lack of transparency, and greenwashing \cite{unm2023}. A thorough understanding of these limitations is therefore essential to develop effective solutions, particularly regarding verification mechanisms, transparency requirements, and organizational controls that can support double-counting prevention \cite{federalregister2023}.

Emerging technologies, such as blockchain and distributed ledger technologies (DLTs), are being explored as potential solutions to address these challenges. These technologies offer the promise of a more transparent and verifiable way to track and verify carbon credits, potentially enhancing the overall integrity of the markets \cite{arxiv2024}.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section II formalizes the relationship between identity fragmentation and double-counting risks in multi-chain carbon credit registries. Section III describes the research methodology, including the systematic literature review and the iterative design process. Section IV provides the technical and conceptual background on carbon markets, fraud vectors, blockchain credentials, and double counting. Section V presents the literature review and identifies the main gaps addressed by this work. Section VI introduces the proposed decentralized identity framework, detailing its system architecture, and the smart contract characteristics. Section VII reports the evaluation results, including gas consumption analysis and a discussion of the framework’s implications for identity-related risk reduction. Finally, Section VIII concludes the paper, summarizing the contributions, limitations, and directions for future research.
